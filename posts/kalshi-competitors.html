Kalshi Competitors

The short answer

Polymarket is Kalshi's closest and most direct rival — the two are frequently compared head-to-head, and both are now CFTC-regulated in the US, which has narrowed a gap that used to clearly favor Kalshi. Beyond Polymarket, Kalshi's competitive landscape splits into a few different lanes: crypto-native exchanges like Crypto.com and OG.com that have added event contracts alongside their core business, play-money forecasting platforms like Manifold, narrower political-only markets like PredictIt, and — increasingly — traditional sportsbooks that overlap with Kalshi specifically in the sports category. Which one actually matters as an alternative depends on why someone is using Kalshi in the first place.

Polymarket: the closest head-to-head rival

Polymarket is the largest prediction market by overall trading volume and, for years, operated as a crypto-native, offshore platform while Kalshi held the clearer US-regulated position. That gap has narrowed significantly: Polymarket relaunched a CFTC-regulated US arm after resolving its earlier regulatory dispute, putting the two platforms on more comparable regulatory footing than at any point in their history. The remaining differences are more about structure and focus than legality. Kalshi settles directly in dollars through standard bank rails and has built particularly deep liquidity in mainstream US sports and political contracts. Polymarket's global platform still leans more crypto-native, settling in USDC, and tends to carry the widest breadth of niche and international markets — tournament-scale global events where Polymarket often has the deepest single-market liquidity anywhere in the industry. If someone's comparing the two head-to-head, the practical question is usually whether they want dollar settlement and depth on mainstream US categories, or broader global and niche coverage with a more crypto-native flow.

Crypto.com and OG.com: exchanges branching into event contracts

Rather than being purpose-built prediction markets, platforms like Crypto.com and OG.com added event-contract trading as an extension of an existing crypto exchange business. For users who already hold funds or trade regularly on one of these platforms, having event contracts available in the same app removes a step compared to funding a separate, dedicated account elsewhere. Depth and market variety on these platforms generally trail purpose-built prediction markets like Kalshi and Polymarket, since event contracts are a secondary feature rather than the core product.

Manifold: play money instead of real stakes

Manifold runs entirely on play money, which sidesteps the regulatory questions that constrain Kalshi's real-money sports and political contracts in certain states, but also removes the financial stakes that drive sharp, disciplined pricing. Its real strength is breadth and speed — users can create a market on almost any question, including ones far too niche or fast-moving for a regulated exchange to list. For someone who wants to test a forecasting instinct without financial risk, or track a question no real-money platform covers yet, Manifold fills that gap; it isn't a substitute for Kalshi's real-money, regulated contracts.

PredictIt: narrower, political-only, real money

PredictIt operates under a research-focused regulatory framework limited mostly to political contracts, with per-market investment caps for any individual trader. It doesn't compete with Kalshi on breadth — Kalshi covers sports, economics, weather, and more alongside politics — but its long operating history in the political-forecasting niche specifically still draws a dedicated user base that predates Kalshi's rise in that category.

Traditional sportsbooks: the newest source of overlap

As Kalshi's sports contracts have grown into one of its largest categories, the platform increasingly competes directly with traditional regulated sportsbooks in states where both operate, rather than only with other prediction markets. The products are structurally different — a sportsbook sets fixed odds and takes the other side of your bet, while Kalshi runs a peer-to-peer exchange with no house edge — but for a bettor deciding where to place money on a game, that structural distinction often matters less than practical factors like liquidity on the specific bet they want, contract or bet-slip design, and which states allow which product. This overlap is also central to the ongoing legal disputes in several states, where regulators have argued Kalshi's sports contracts function enough like sports betting to fall under state gambling law regardless of federal derivatives classification.

How to actually pick between them

If dollar settlement and deep liquidity on mainstream US sports and politics matter most, Kalshi remains the more traditional, straightforward choice. If broader global and niche market coverage matters more, or a crypto-native settlement flow is preferred, Polymarket's global platform still has the widest breadth. If an existing crypto exchange account makes convenience the priority, Crypto.com or OG.com's event-contract offerings remove a step, with the tradeoff of shallower markets. If the goal is testing forecasting skill without financial risk, Manifold is the low-stakes option. And for a trader focused specifically and only on political outcomes, PredictIt's long-established niche still has a following, position caps notwithstanding.
